---
name: On-call escalation ladder and the rule about waking the second responder
description: The severity definitions, how long a primary responder should hold an incident before escalating, and the explicit permission to escalate early that this document exists to grant.
type: policy
---
There are four severity levels and they are defined by customer impact rather than by how alarming the alert looked. The highest is reserved for a complete loss of a core function or any confirmed exposure of customer data, and it pages immediately with no acknowledgement window. The second covers a severe degradation that a customer would notice and complain about, such as a function working for a minority of requests, and it pages during any hour. The third covers degradation with a workaround or impact confined to internal users, and it pages during working hours and queues outside them. The fourth is a tracked defect with no live impact and never pages.

The classification is a first guess and is expected to change. Incidents are routinely opened at the third level and raised to the second once someone understands the blast radius, and that is the system working rather than a mistake. The important consequence is that the initial classification is not a commitment, so there is no reason to agonise over it at the start.

The primary responder acknowledges within five minutes and holds the incident for thirty, and that thirty minute figure is the number this document exists to argue about. It is a ceiling, not a target. If after thirty minutes of work you do not have a working theory of the cause, escalate to the secondary. The reason for a ceiling is that the most expensive incidents in our history were not the ones that were hard, they were the ones where a single responder spent two hours on a theory that was wrong and nobody else looked at it. A second person arrives with no sunk cost and no attachment to the first theory, and that is worth far more than their familiarity with the system.

This document grants explicit permission to escalate earlier than thirty minutes for any reason, including reasons that are not technical. Escalate if the incident is growing rather than shrinking. Escalate if it touches a system you have not worked on. Escalate if you are tired, if you were asleep twenty minutes ago, or if you simply have a bad feeling about it. Nobody has ever been criticised here for escalating early and the expectation is that it happens often. If you find yourself weighing whether the problem is big enough to justify waking someone, that deliberation is itself the signal: escalate.

The secondary responder acknowledges within fifteen minutes. If they do not, the page goes to the engineering manager on the rotation, and if that is also unacknowledged after fifteen minutes it goes to the whole team channel with an audible alert. Escalation beyond the team happens for the two highest severities only, and is the incident commander's call rather than the responder's. Confirmed data exposure is the one case with a mandatory path regardless of severity judgement: it goes to the security and legal contacts within one hour of confirmation, because the clock on the external notification obligation starts at confirmation and not at resolution.
